# Final results (title-grouped film split)

Generated by `bgm report --final` from runs/titlesplit/ (src/bgm/eval/final_film.py). 1,248 pairs; folds over film groups merged by **movie title** (`data/manifests/folds_title.json`, D102: SunNXT / YouTube uploads of one movie are one group; 219 groups, 228 movies): no film is in both training and test; Konjum salangai (266 clips) is train-only, so the test folds hold the other 982 clips. Test data never touches training, early stopping, calibration, fusion weights or penalties. Encoders frozen; SigLIP so400m video, CLAP (`larger_clap_music_and_speech`, D1) + MERT-v1-330M music.

**Final system (D75):** a 10-head ensemble of SigLIP → CLAP+MERT semantic heads (mean cosine), semantic-only coarse-to-fine retrieval, Viterbi stitching with penalties tuned on val concatenations. Dynamics, mood, whole-video context, person branch and the cue-switch predictor are off: none gave a significant gain. Ensemble runs: 15 retrieval, 15 pipeline (5 folds × 3 disjoint 10-head ensembles).

Tables: mean ± std over runs. Comparisons: paired, per-query values averaged over seeds, 95% cluster bootstrap over film groups, 10,000 resamples (concatenations resampled as units). The earlier track_id-split report, whose test folds share films with training, is results/final_trackid.md.

## 5. Offset precision of the final system's top-1 window

For each test segment: the top-1 (file, offset) after the coarse and fine stages. A hit needs the file in the right track group AND the offset within the tolerance of the true (aligned) offset; windows tied at the top share the hit. Chance is exact per query: the share of all gallery windows (every test-fold file, every 0.5 s) that the same rule accepts.

| rule | coarse-to-fine | chance | minus chance [95% CI] | verdict | no coarse stage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| right track group, any offset | 0.041 ± 0.012 | 0.009 | +0.032 [+0.022, +0.042] | above chance | 0.042 ± 0.012 |
| right track group and within ±1 s | 0.013 ± 0.006 | 0.001 | +0.012 [+0.009, +0.016] | above chance | 0.013 ± 0.006 |
| right track group and within ±3 s | 0.020 ± 0.007 | 0.002 | +0.018 [+0.013, +0.023] | above chance | 0.020 ± 0.007 |
| right track group and within ±5 s | 0.025 ± 0.008 | 0.003 | +0.021 [+0.016, +0.028] | above chance | 0.025 ± 0.008 |

The joint numbers are mostly track retrieval: the chance levels are tiny because a random window is rarely in the right track group at all. The next table asks the offset question on its own. Among the segments whose top-1 file is in the right track group, how often is the offset within the tolerance, against a random window of that track group's files?

| tolerance | within tolerance, given the right track group | chance, given the right track group | minus chance [95% CI] | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ±1 s | 0.323 | 0.233 | +0.091 [+0.038, +0.150] | above chance |
| ±3 s | 0.487 | 0.407 | +0.081 [+0.023, +0.143] | above chance |
| ±5 s | 0.609 | 0.555 | +0.055 [+0.002, +0.112] | marginally above chance |

Once the right track group is found, the offset is within ±1 s 0.323 of the time against 0.233 for a random window of it. The three rows are nested and share the same segments, so they are one finding, not three independent tests: the offset is only slightly better than random.

Context for the chance levels: segments last 4.9 s (median) and the files are short, so a file offers few distinct windows and a wide tolerance covers most of them. 104 of 2,552 segments (seed-averaged) have their top-1 in the right track group.
