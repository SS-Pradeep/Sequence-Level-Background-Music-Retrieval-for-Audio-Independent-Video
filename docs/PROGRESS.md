  `transition_cost` in stitch.py). Two excerpts of one track get the same key and tempo even if the music modulates.
- **Proposal:** for a switch from candidate c = (k, τ, d) to c′ = (k′, τ′), compare the key / tempo of the last ~4–8 s of
  c's window, [τ + d − w, τ + d], with the first ~4–8 s of c′'s, [τ′, τ′ + w]. Lattice layers would then store
  `key_end`, `tempo_end`, `key_start`, `tempo_start` per candidate instead of one key and tempo; the cost formula and
  Viterbi stay the same.
- **Features:** tempo per window can come from the cached `beat_times_s` (median inter-beat interval inside the window;
  no re-extraction). Key needs chroma per second, which is not cached: new librosa chroma at 22,050 Hz from the interim
  FLACs, under a new config hash.
- **Caveats:** the music-side signals are weak at track level already (librosa tempo estimators agree within 5% on
  ~40% of tracks; chroma key is the same across a track's 8 s windows 46% of the time), and shorter windows will be
  noisier. The val-tuned μ is 0 in 7 of the 15 title-split runs, 0.5 in 5 and 1.0 in 3
  (runs/titlesplit/pipeline/fold*/seed*/stitch.json), so the term does little now. A neural beat tracker / key estimator, checked on a few hand-labelled clips, may be needed
  before per-window values help.
- **Test, if run:** μ re-tuned on val with per-window vs per-track values, same lattices; switch F1, part track accuracy
  and the key / tempo distance at the chosen seams on the test concatenations, film-group bootstrap.

## Open issue: periodic dips in the motion curve

`log_motion` drops sharply on 5–14% of frames at regular intervals: every 24–25 frames for 23.976 / 24 fps sources (the
frame duplicated once per second by the 25 fps conversion) and every 5 frames for 25 / 29.97 / 30 fps sources. Flow
between near-identical frames is ~0, so these are artifacts, not motion. They inject a periodic signal into log_motion
and visual_onset (dynamics branch, hand-crafted baseline, energy-match check). Proposed fix, not applied: flag
near-duplicate frames and interpolate motion across them (or compute flow at the native frame rate and resample to 25 Hz),
then re-extract dyn_video and retrain M7 and rerun M9–M10. **Approved and applied 2026-09-26 (D27).**
